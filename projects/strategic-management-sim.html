Fall 2025 · Group Project

Strategic Management Simulation

A multi-week business simulation in Globstrat where, as a team of 5, we made strategic decision for a company through 6 simulated years on a market with 9 direct competitors (other teams).

KPI Tracking Written Analysis Teamwork

The setup

Platform

Globstrat business simulation software — real decision rounds, real competitive dynamics between teams

Auditing

Initial group strategy presentation, weekly individual presentations on assigned topics, final group presentation to all classmates

My role

R&D Director, with acting Vice-CEO responsibilities when needed

Turning point 1 - Trial Round Misalignment

Early on, we each focused on our own department, put numbers into Globstrat, and assumed that was enough. It led to misalignment across the team and a rough first result. That failure pushed me to read the documentation for every role: HR, marketing, finance - not just R&D. Understanding how the whole company worked changed how I approached every decision after that.

Turning point 2 - acting CEO day

Our CEO had an exam on a decision day, and I was chosen to cover. I expected to check the inputs and click submit. Instead I found myself negotiating alliances with other teams and reorganising our entire budget from scratch. I was alone, with no time to consult teammates. It was overwhelming, but it forced me to use everything I'd learned about the company as a system, not just my own segment. I also managed to find room in the budget for a joint venture after all decisions were already in, using the same analytical judgment I apply in my technical work.

The strategic positioning insight

My individual presentation covered competitive positioning. Using Blue Ocean Strategy as a framework, I mapped where each team sat in the market — and argued that we needed to take a riskier path rather than compete on the same dimensions as everyone else.

Where we were

Red ocean

Competing directly on the same product dimensions as most other teams — limited differentiation, real margin pressure.

Where one competitor was

Blue ocean

One team had found an uncontested space. Getting there fully wasn't realistic mid-simulation.

My recommendation

Blue patch

Skip one innovation step to leapfrog competitors — higher risk, but the only credible route to partial differentiation from our position.

The team adopted the recommendation. Arriving at a more innovative product ahead of teams that followed the standard progression paid off most visibly in the enterprise segment, where we ended the simulation with the highest market share across all competing teams.

Outcomes

#1 enterprise market share across all 10 competing teams
#3 share value among similar-profile companies out of 9 teams
↑ CSR steady growth in CSR index, as planned in initial strategy
↑ Q steady growth in Quality index throughout the simulation

What I took away

This course gave me something my technical degree hadn't: the experience of making consequential decisions in areas I didn't fully know, under time pressure, without the safety net of a correct answer. I used my risk management knowledge to push for differentiation while keeping a safety net in place — the same instinct I apply when designing an experiment or choosing a model. The main lesson was that the broader your understanding of a system, the better your analysis of any part of it. That applies to companies as much as it does to data.
